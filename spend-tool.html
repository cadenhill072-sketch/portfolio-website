Sourcing internship, packaging direct materials, summer 2026

Packaging spend in one view

Spend, drawings and technical reports for packaging items lived in separate places. I built a tool that brings them together, so sourcing and R&D can look at a whole family of items at once.

Rebuilt for this portfolio and privatized. The diagram and demo use invented families, suppliers and shares. No company data, files, screenshots, supplier names or internal system names appear on this page.

The problem

Tail spend is the long list of items that each cost too little to get much attention on their own. In packaging, the useful questions about those items are simple to ask and slow to answer. Who makes this, does anyone else make the same thing, and is there a drawing on file?

Answering them takes three kinds of information that sit in different places: what was spent, what the packaging drawing shows, and what the technical report says about the structure. Without them side by side, it's hard to see that two items from two suppliers are really the same film.

What I built

I built a reusable web tool with enterprise generative AI that pulls the three together. Items are matched by their packaging structure and grouped into families, so each family shows its total spend, which suppliers make it, and whether a drawing exists.

It isn't tied to one category. The same approach runs on any packaging category, which was the point of building it as a tool instead of a one-off analysis.

From a family to a decision

Each family gets a proposed next step, along with how easy it would be, how confident the evidence is, the risks, and the questions R&D would need to answer before anyone acts.

If several suppliers make the same structure, the move is to consolidate, so one award builds volume. If a qualified alternative already exists, a mini-bid tests price with a small competitive event. If there's no realistic alternative yet, the lever is a negotiation with the current supplier.
