About

The problems I like best are the ones where the data is a mess and the stakes are concrete: a packaging category nobody has looked at in years, or five years of bid files nobody can search.

I am a junior at the University of Kansas studying finance and supply chain management with a minor in data analytics, graduating in May 2027. Most of what I know how to do I learned by being handed a pile of unstructured data and a question somebody actually needed answered.

Summer 2026 I was a sourcing analyst intern on the packaging direct materials team at General Mills, working on film and flexible/laminate categories. The summer before that I was an operations intern at TA Dedicated, a dedicated freight carrier, building reporting tools for the sales and pricing teams. During the school year I am the head teaching assistant for SCM 310, KU's core supply chain course, where I coordinate the TA team and act as the bridge between the instructor and about 200 students. I also joined the leadership team of KU's Supply Chain Management Club for 2026–27.

My toolkit is Power BI, Python, and Excel for the analysis, plus spend analysis and supplier management on the sourcing side. I am currently looking for full-time procurement, sourcing, and supply chain analytics roles starting after graduation.

Internship case study

Packaging spend intelligence tool

Sourcing and R&D teams evaluating a packaging category had to pull spend from one system, technical drawings from another, and material specs from a third. I built a web-based tool using enterprise generative AI that brings all three into a single interface, so a question like "what are we paying across suppliers for this laminate structure" gets answered in one place instead of five. The linked demo is the real tool with every employer, supplier, material and brand identifier replaced and all dollar values scaled — the structure and logic are intact, the proprietary data is not.

Python · Power BI

Searchable carrier bid repository

A freight carrier's five years of customer bid files sat in 55 GB of spreadsheets that nobody could search, so the sales team was quoting new business blind. I wrote a Python pipeline to parse and normalize the files into a single structured dataset and surfaced it through Power BI, giving reps lane-level history at the point of the conversation. I later extended the same pattern to file incoming carrier bids automatically for pricing review. The case study rebuilds the dashboard with invented data and links to the parsing code, which ships with a generator for its own synthetic bid files.

Project leadership

A period game set for a heritage house

A local heritage house wanted something visitors could handle rather than look at. I researched period games, designed the pieces in SketchUp, priced and partly donated the bill of materials, hand-built the set including steam-bent wooden hoops, and delivered it with written instructions. Sixty logged hours of my own time over eleven months, plus a volunteer build day I scoped and ran. It was the first supply chain problem I ever solved, before I knew that is what it was called.
