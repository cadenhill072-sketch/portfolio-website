Eagle Scout service project, 2018 to 2019

Period yard games for the Heritage House

I led a group of Scouts building period yard games and a storage chest for the Woodbury Heritage Society's Heritage House. The project took more than 80 hours, and the games were used at the Society's open house in August 2019.

The ask

The house shows what early American life looked like to school groups and visitors, and it wanted something hands-on that kids could pick up and play with on the lawn. That was the whole brief, so the rest was mine to figure out.

The project started with an email to the Heritage House in July 2018. I researched which games were actually played in the period, then pitched the project to the house in January 2019 and to my troop's leadership council in February, so we agreed on what I'd make before I bought any wood.

Design and sourcing

I modeled the pieces in SketchUp before settling on dimensions. The hoops had to be a size a kid could actually catch, and the chest had to hold everything without the hoops warping against each other. The drawings went through a couple of rounds with my project coach.

I priced a bill of materials line by line, then covered part of it through donation requests to local businesses. Asking for donations against a specific, priced list went a lot better than asking for general help, and it was the first time I saw how much of a good sourcing ask is preparation.

A lot of my time went in before any wood was cut, between the research, the design and the sourcing.

Bending the wood

The hoops are steam-bent. A 37-inch strip goes into a soak, then into steam, then onto a form and into clamps, and there's only a short window where it bends without cracking. My first session ran close to four hours between soaking and clamping, mostly waiting and then moving fast.

What made it work was building the form first and testing a scrap piece before using the good stock. I learned that on the second try instead of the first.

Running the build day

The group build day on April 13, 2019 is the part an Eagle project really tests. It ran five and a half hours, and I spent about four hours beforehand cutting and prepping stock, so the Scouts showed up to defined tasks instead of a pile of lumber. On the day, Scouts at different skill levels worked at the same time, and I sequenced the jobs so nobody was stuck waiting on me.

I still think about that in supply chain work. A plan only works when the work is broken down small enough for someone else to pick up.

What I'd do differently

I'd build the bending form and run a scrap test before the first real piece, which would have saved most of a session. I'd also write the game instructions early instead of at the end, since writing them forced decisions about the pieces that I'd rather have made while I could still change the design.

I delivered the set to the Heritage House on June 9, 2019, and earned the Eagle Scout rank in July 2020.
